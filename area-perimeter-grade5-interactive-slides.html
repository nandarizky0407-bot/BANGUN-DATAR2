    🟠 Concept 1 • Perimeter

    🟠 What is Perimeter?

    📌 Perimeter is the distance around the outside edge of a shape.
    🧠 The word comes from Greek: peri = around, meter = measure. So perimeter literally means “measure around”!
    🐜 Watch the ant walk ONE full trip around the rectangle!
    🐜
    🧮 Distance walked: 0.0 m

    Press Walk and watch the odometer count every metre the ant travels along the edge. When it returns to the start, the odometer shows the perimeter of the rectangle!

    🎉 One full trip around = 40 m. That is the PERIMETER!

    🔑 Key idea

    To find the perimeter of any shape, ADD the lengths of ALL its sides. For our rectangle: 12 + 8 + 12 + 8 = 40 m.

    📏 We measure perimeter in units of length: centimetres (cm), metres (m), kilometres (km).

    🟠 Skill 1 • Calculating

    🟠 How to Find the Perimeter

    ▭ Rectangle

    P = 2 × (length + width)
    or P = l + w + l + w

    A rectangle has two equal lengths and two equal widths, so we can add them once and double it.

    Example: length = 9 cm, width = 4 cm
    P = 2 × (9 + 4) = 2 × 13 = 26 cm

    □ Square

    P = 4 × side

    A square has 4 equal sides, so we simply multiply one side by 4!

    Example: side = 6 m
    P = 4 × 6 = 24 m
    👀 Look at the sides

    9 + 4 + 9 + 4 = 26 cm ✓

    4 × 6 = 24 m ✓

    💡 Always write the unit (cm, m, km) in your answer. An answer without a unit is like a cake without icing!
    🟢 Concept 2 • Area

    🟢 What is Area?

    📌 Area is the amount of surface inside a shape. It tells us how much space the shape covers.
    📐 We measure area in SQUARE UNITS, such as cm² (square centimetres) or m² (square metres). One square metre is the size of a square with sides of 1 metre.
    👆 Click the squares to cover the rectangle — then count them!
    🟩 Squares placed: 0

    This rectangle is 6 units long and 4 units wide. When you cover it completely, you will use 24 unit squares!

    🎉 24 unit squares cover the shape, so its AREA is 24 square units!

    🔑 Key idea

    Area = how many unit squares fit inside the shape. Instead of counting one by one, mathematicians found a shortcut… (next slide!)

    🟢 Skill 2 • Calculating

    🟢 How to Find the Area

    🧠 Why multiply? Think in rows!

    Our 6 × 4 rectangle has 4 rows of squares, and every row contains 6 squares. Instead of counting 6 + 6 + 6 + 6, we can multiply:

    6 × 4 = 24 squares

    ▭ Rectangle

    A = length × width
    Example: length = 7 cm, width = 5 cm
    A = 7 × 5 = 35 cm²

    □ Square

    A = side × side
    Example: side = 9 m
    A = 9 × 9 = 81 m²
    ⚠️ Do not mix them up! Perimeter uses units of length (cm, m). Area uses square units (cm², m²). Perimeter is the fence 🚧 — area is the grass 🌱!

    🟠🔍 Skill 3 • Working Backwards

    🟠🔍 Find the Missing Side (Perimeter Known)

    Sometimes we know the perimeter but one side is a mystery! Detectives work backwards.

    □ For a square

    side = P ÷ 4

    All 4 sides are equal, so share the perimeter equally among the 4 sides.

    ▭ For a rectangle

    l + w = P ÷ 2

    Halve the perimeter to get length + width, then subtract the side you already know.

    🪜 Guided example — step by step

    A rectangle has a perimeter of 28 cm. Its length is 8 cm. What is its width?
    1Halve the perimeter to find length + width:
    P ÷ 2 = 28 ÷ 2 = 14 cm → so length + width = 14 cm.
    2Subtract the side you know (the length):
    14 − 8 = 6 cm.
    3Check it forwards: P = 2 × (8 + 6) = 2 × 14 = 28 ✓
    The width is 6 cm! 🎉
    ✏️ Your turn, detective!
    🟢🔍 Skill 4 • Working Backwards

    🟢🔍 Find the Missing Side (Area Known)

    Now the area is known, but a side is missing. Multiplication has a superhero partner: division!

    ▭ For a rectangle

    w = A ÷ l

    Because A = l × w, division undoes the multiplication: split the area into equal rows!

    □ For a square

    s × s = A

    Ask: what number times itself gives the area? Use your multiplication facts!

    🪜 Guided example — step by step

    A rectangle has an area of 24 cm². Its length is 6 cm. What is its width?
    1Write what you know: A = l × w, so 24 = 6 × w. The area is 6 rows of w squares each.
    2Divide to undo the multiplication:
    w = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 cm.
    3Check it forwards: A = 6 × 4 = 24 cm² ✓
    The width is 4 cm! 🎉
    ✏️ Your turn, detective!

    🌟 What Have We Learned?

    🌟 Conclusion

    🟠 Perimeter 🟢 Area
    What it measures Distance AROUND the edge 🚧 Surface COVERED inside 🌱
    How to find it ADD all the sides ➕ MULTIPLY length × width ✖️
    Formula (rectangle) P = 2 × (l + w) A = l × w
    Formula (square) P = 4 × s A = s × s
    Units length units: cm, m square units: cm², m²
    🧠Big idea 1: Perimeter is a one-dimensional walk around the shape — like an ant 🐜 walking along the fence. Area is two-dimensional — like grass 🌱 covering the field.
    🔍Big idea 2: Missing side? Work backwards! Square: s = P ÷ 4. Rectangle: l + w = P ÷ 2, then subtract. With area: w = A ÷ l (and think “what times itself gives A?” for squares).
    🌍Big idea 3: Perimeter and area are everywhere — fencing gardens, tiling floors, framing pictures, wrapping presents. Math helps us plan the real world!
