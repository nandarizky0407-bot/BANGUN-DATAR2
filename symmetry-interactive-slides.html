Polygons everywhere!

Before we meet symmetry, let's warm up with the shapes we already know.

What is a polygon?

A polygon is a closed 2D shape made of straight sides. No curves allowed — sorry, circles!

Polygons get their names from their number of sides: 3 sides make a triangle, 4 a quadrilateral, 5 a pentagon… all the way up!

Regular polygon: all sides are equal and all angles are equal. Regular polygons are the most symmetric shapes of all!
Irregular polygon: its sides or angles are different from each other.

The amazing circle

Circles are not polygons, but they are the superstars of symmetry. Explore it yourself!

Explore the circle

Circle facts

  • A circle is a round shape where every point is the same distance from the centre.
  • The radius goes from the centre to the edge. The diameter goes all the way across through the centre — it equals 2 radii!
  • A circle is not a polygon: it has one curved side and no straight edges or corners.

The symmetry champion

Every diameter of a circle is a line of symmetry — fold along any diameter and the halves always match. A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry. No other shape comes close!

Reflective symmetry

Fold it to see it!

A shape has reflective symmetry if you can fold it along a line so that both halves match exactly. That folding line is called the line of symmetry (or mirror line).

Rotational symmetry

What does it mean?

A shape has rotational symmetry if it looks exactly the same after a turn around its centre point.

The order of rotational symmetry tells you how many times the shape looks the same in one full turn.

Remember the parallelogram? It has no lines of symmetry… but it DOES have rotational symmetry of order 2! Try it on the spinner →
Handy rule: for a regular polygon, the order of rotational symmetry always equals its number of sides.

Let's sum upKey ideas

What we learned today

Reflective symmetry

A shape with reflective symmetry can be folded along its line of symmetry so both halves match exactly. A square has 4 lines, a rectangle has 2 — and a circle has infinitely many!

Rotational symmetry

A shape with rotational symmetry looks the same after a turn around its centre. The order is how many times it matches in one full turn — a regular hexagon has order 6!

Regular polygons

Regular polygons have equal sides and equal angles. A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry and rotational order n. A handy rule to remember!

Tricksters to remember

A parallelogram has rotational symmetry (order 2) but no lines of symmetry. A scalene triangle has no symmetry at all. And the circle breaks every rule — infinitely many lines!
